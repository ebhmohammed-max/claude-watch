## §1 · WHAT THIS IS, AND WHAT IT IS FOR

### 1.1 The one-sentence thesis

> **Short-form video is not a content problem. It is a hook problem, a clarity problem, and a consistency problem — in that order.**

Almost everyone who fails at this fails at the first one and blames the third.

### 1.2 The three things that actually matter

From `[COURSE]` M04 L05 — the production sequence has a **blocking gate** at every stage. You do not proceed to the next stage until the current one passes. This is the single most important structural idea in the course, and almost nobody follows it.

```
PRE-SCRIPTING → SCRIPTING → RECORDING → EDITING → POSTING → ANALYSING
     ↑ gate         ↑ gate       ↑ gate      ↑ gate     ↑ gate
```

A video with a weak hook that was beautifully edited is a **failed video**. The gate at `SCRIPTING` should have caught it. Gate discipline is what separates a channel that compounds from a channel that produces.

### 1.3 The funnel logic that explains everything

`[COURSE]` M08 L02 — short-form is **fed, not chosen.** The viewer does not pick your video; an algorithm inserts it into a stream of things they did not ask for. This has a brutal consequence:

> **Everything after a churn point might as well have been a black screen.**

If 80% leave in the first 3 seconds, then 80% of your production effort was spent on an audience that never existed. This is why the hook gets 30–40% of total effort (see §5) and why it is not a stylistic preference but an arithmetic one.

## §3 · POSITIONING AND NICHE

### 3.1 The trust equation `[COURSE]` M05 L02

```
Trust        = Value
Value        = Usefulness + Novelty + Compelling
Trust/week   = Value × #Posts × #Views
```

**It is multiplicative.** This is the whole point. Zero posts means zero trust, no matter how good the value is. Zero value means zero trust, no matter how many posts. There is no compensation between the terms.

The practical consequence: **volume with adequate value beats perfection with no volume.** The equation is a product, and a zero anywhere kills it — but so does a near-zero.

### 3.2 Cult-like positioning `[COURSE]` M05 L02

Build a "cult-like personal brand" in **one category**: cult-like fandom plus demonstrated authority.

**The test:** when an on-target buyer hits a problem, your name pops up first. That is the only positioning test that matters — not whether you are clear, not whether you are memorable, but whether you are *the first name retrieved* at the moment of need.

### 3.3 IVA — the Ideal Viewer Avatar `[COURSE]` M05 L04, M09 L07

Everything is written for one person. Not a demographic — a **specific person with a specific problem at a specific moment.** The course's idea-development prompt: find the most interesting angle on what you know to be true **that most of your IVAs will not** know or believe.

That single clause — *that most of your IVA won't* — is the difference between content that teaches and content that confirms.

### 3.4 Useful vs interesting `[COURSE]` M07 L05

| | Interesting | Useful |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | Contrast between expectation and reality, quickly believable | Tactically actionable, immediately implementable, non-obvious |
| Earns | attention | retention, saves, trust |
| Failure | clickbait; empty after the reveal | skimmed; no reason to stop |

**You want both.** Interesting gets them to stop; useful gets them to stay and come back.

### 3.5 Viewer vs buyer `[COURSE]` M05 L02

- **Viewers** see value as *something interesting.*
- **Buyers** see value as *solutions.*

A channel of pure interestingness builds an audience that will never buy. A channel of pure solutions never gets found. The mix has to be deliberate.

### 3.6 The niche-research method `[COURSE]` M05 L04

1. **Find 10–20 profiles** serving the exact same IVA.
2. **Micro / small / medium only. Maximum 50% medium.**
3. **Exclude mega accounts.** Their hits are contaminated by accumulated fandom — you cannot tell whether the video worked or the person's existing audience did.

**The anchor-account method:** follow one account → press "Following" → mine the 300–1000 handpicked accounts it follows → repeat. Plus your own list, Explore, and platform search.

## §4 · IDEA RESEARCH

### 4.1 The six idea sources, ranked `[COURSE]` M07 L03

1. **Validated outliers — your own channel.** Best signal you will ever get.
2. **Validated outliers — short-form, other channels.**
3. **Validated outliers — other mediums** (long-form, newsletters, podcasts).
4. **Category news / blogs / AI search.**
5. **Non-outliers.**
6. **Originals.**

**Originals last, and that is not a limitation — it is a strategy.** There is no better signal for de-risking effort than a validated outlier. Copying one is not a guarantee, but it is the best odds available, all else held equal.

### 4.2 What a validated outlier actually is `[COURSE]` M07 L03

> **A video that beat its own channel's baseline — e.g. 119× — not a view count.**

This distinction is the most misunderstood thing in the discipline. A video with 2M views on a channel that averages 1.8M views is **not** an outlier. It is normal. A video with 40K views on a channel that averages 2K views is a screaming outlier and worth ten times more to you.

### 4.3 Non-outliers are still usable `[COURSE]` M07 L03

A non-outlier does not mean the idea cannot work. It means **the first person could not make it work.** Different claim, entirely different conclusion.

### 4.4 The idea-development time-box `[COURSE]` M07 L07

**30 minutes. One idea. Notes only.**

Not scripting. Not outlining. Notes. The time-box exists because idea quality collapses when you let it expand into production; you start optimising the thing you already have instead of finding the better thing you don't.

### 4.5 Building a topic bank for any niche `[DERIVED]`

The method used to build a 104-topic bank for one channel, generalised:

1. **Cluster the niche's recurring problems** — 10–12 clusters, named as the *viewer's* problem, not the topic's category.
2. **For each cluster, write 8–10 topics** as hook + payoff pairs. The payoff is one nugget, not a topic area.
3. **Tag every topic with a format** (§7) and an **evidence tag**:
   - `LIVED` — you have done this and have a receipt
   - `STANCE` — you believe it; no receipt yet
   - `PUBLIC` — a documented third-party case; the source must be cited on screen
4. **Audit the whole bank** against two tests: *is this a clone of someone else?* and *have I made this video already?*
5. **Expect the ratio to embarrass you.** A healthy first-draft bank is mostly `STANCE`. That is what a bank looks like when it is written ahead of the evidence.
6. **Fix the format mix, not the topic count.** If 48% of your bank is one format, you have a single-format channel with 104 episodes, not range.

**The proof engine** `[DERIVED]` — the fix for a `STANCE`-heavy bank:

> **Do not teach a thing until you have done it that week.**

Pick one behavioural topic per week. Do it. Film the result. This converts `STANCE`→`LIVED` at roughly one per week, manufactures the receipts as a by-product of running the channel, and makes every video structurally un-copyable because the story is the moat.

## §5 · HOOKS — the 30–40% of your effort

### 5.1 The four components, ranked `[COURSE]` M08 L11, M08 L02

> **Text → Visual → Spoken → Audio**

Text and visuals are absorbed faster than sound. A viewer reads the caption before they hear the first word, and sees the frame before either.

**The number that should govern your career:** maximum alignment across all four components versus misalignment is the difference between **500 and 500,000 views.** Same content. Same voice. Different hook discipline.

> ⚠️ Note a documented contradiction in the source: M08 L02's "Hooks 101" doc presents **three** components; M08 L11's workshop presents **four**. The four-component version is the more complete one and is what is used here. Recorded, not reconciled.

### 5.2 The five Hook Viewer Retention Levers `[COURSE]` M07 L05

1. **Raw intrigue** — a question the viewer cannot close without watching.
2. **Raw shock** — must be *"not outlandish enough to dismiss."* Shock that reads as fake gets scrolled. The bar is: surprising but credible.
3. **Perceived deep value** — laser-targeted at the IVA. Not general value; *their* value.
4. **Emotional transfer** — the viewer adopts your feeling about the subject.
5. **Impulse to engage** — downstream of the other four. It is an effect, not a lever. If you have to ask for engagement in the first three seconds, the first four failed.

### 5.3 The three psychology drivers `[COURSE]` M07 L05

- **Dream outcome** — moving toward something wanted
- **Intrigue / curiosity** — the information gap
- **Nightmare scenario** — fear of losing, or protecting what they have

**The most neglected driver is the third.** Almost everyone writes dream-outcome content. Loss aversion is roughly twice as motivating and almost nobody in a given niche uses it — which is exactly why it works.

### 5.4 Contrast — the engine underneath `[COURSE]` M08 L11, M09 L03

> **Contrast = the distance between expectation and reality.**

| Type | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Stated** contrast | Clearer, but *narrows* — it tells the viewer exactly what the video is about |
| **Implied** contrast | Wider, but risks being missed |
| **Maximum contrast** | 100 people hold one belief and only you hold the other |

The strongest hook in the discipline is the one where the audience is unanimous and you are alone. That is uncomfortable, and that is why it works.

### 5.5 The leash `[COURSE]` M09 L03

> **Curiosity runway = how much they care about the pain × how uncertain they are of their belief.**

Two ways to kill it, both common:
- **Confidence** — if they are sure, there is no gap to close.
- **Indifference** — if they do not care, the gap is not worth closing.

You are looking for the viewer who cares a lot and is unsure. That is a narrower audience than "everyone," and it converts far better.

### 5.6 The five-step hook method `[COURSE]` M09 L03

1. Find the pain point.
2. Map the common belief.
3. State the contrarian reality — **one you genuinely believe.**
4. Frame why it beats their belief.
5. One quick proof.

Step 3 is where people lie. If you do not believe the contrarian claim, the delivery betrays it and the audience can tell. **Write only contrarian claims you actually hold.**

### 5.7 The hook writing process `[COURSE]` M08 L11

```
Audit visuals → spoken hook → complementary visual → text hook → record takes → edit check
```

Note the order: **visuals first.** Not words. The most common error is to write a clever line and then hunt for a picture that fits. The course starts with the picture and writes a line that completes it.

### 5.8 On-target hook `[COURSE]` M08 L11

- Rapid context in sentence one
- Short, simple words
- Minimum words
- **"You / your"** — never "me / I"
- Unmistakable clarity

### 5.9 Eight hook families `[COURSE]` — the taxonomy

`last-24-hours` · `they-erased-this` · `what-if` · `big-number` · `counterintuitive-reveal` · `identity-trigger` · `ranked-list` · `watch-to-end`

### 5.10 A ninth family to use sparingly `[DERIVED]`

Auditing a 104-topic bank revealed a ninth family in wide use and absent from the taxonomy:

> **"You're not X. You're Y."** — *e.g. "You don't have an idea problem. You have a finishing problem."*

It works because it delivers a complete reframe in one breath. It fails when over-used, because the audience learns the shape and starts predicting the second half — at which point the contrast collapses to zero.

**Working rule** `[DERIVED]`: **maximum 3 per 30 posts.** One as the channel's origin statement is fine. Thirteen in a hundred is a formula, and a formula is a liability the moment a viewer notices it.

### 5.11 Hook failure modes

| Failure | Symptom | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **Slow to value** | Value arrives after 5 s | Move the payoff line to sentence one; cut the setup |
| **Bait-and-switch** | Hook promises more than the body delivers | Under-promise on the bait, over-deliver in the body |
| **Jargon hook** | Uses the industry's words, not the viewer's | Rewrite with a 6th-grader's vocabulary |
| **Formula fatigue** | Same construction across many videos | Rotate families deliberately (§5.9) |
| **Visual afterthought** | The picture is decoration, not information | Start from the visual (§5.7) |
| **Unattributed borrowed line** | A named method presented as yours | §14.4 — attribute on screen or rewrite |

## §6 · SCRIPTING

### 6.1 The three-part skeleton `[COURSE]` M09 L04

```
HOOK     →  1–3 lines
BODY     →  8–10 lines, with contrast "headfakes" to re-hook
PAYOFF   →  1–2 lines: payoff + emotional transfer + CTA
```

The **headfake** is the key mechanism. Every ~3 body lines, you re-open a small gap so the viewer cannot settle. Without headfakes, retention decays linearly; with them, it saw-tooths.

### 6.2 The brain pyramid `[COURSE]` M09 L02

```
        problem          ← widest
        stakes
        strategy
        tactics
        examples
        repeated principle ← narrowest
```

**This is the inverse of how experts explain.** An expert opens with the principle and closes with the example. A viewer needs the problem first, or they have no reason to accept the principle.

### 6.3 Concede before you correct `[COURSE]` M09 L02

Affirm their belief where it is right, and use the relief as momentum for the reveal.

> Hook earns attention by contrast. **Body earns absorption by concession.**

A video that is contrarian for 60 seconds straight triggers defence at about second 12. A video that says "you're right about X — and that's exactly why Y" gets through.

### 6.4 Shortest-distance ordering `[COURSE]` M09 L04

Sequence substance by **shortest distance to the preceding line**, not by importance.

This feels wrong the first time you do it. The most important point often belongs in the middle, not the end, because it is closest to what the previous line just set up. Importance-ordering is how essays are written; proximity-ordering is how speech works.

**Corollary:** max **2–3 substance buckets** in a 45–60 s video. More than three and none of them land.

### 6.5 The $100-per-sentence test `[COURSE]` M09 L04, L07

> Include only what you would **pay $100 to say.**

A brutal edit. It removes the competent-but-forgettable sentence, which is the most common filler in a script that has been "cleaned up" rather than cut.

### 6.6 The nine scriptwriting principles `[COURSE]` M09 L03

1. Curiosity & contrast
2. Value density
3. Clarity & comprehension
4. Speed to value
5. Absorption rate
6. Anticipation
7. Emotional resonance
8. Rhythm / cadence & flow
9. Tone & energy

### 6.7 Clarity tactics `[COURSE]` M09 L03

- Sixth-grade words
- Active voice
- Enunciation — **refilm stumbles**, do not edit around them
- **Say key points twice** — once to land, once to stick
- **Compress to the atomic phrase** — the shortest version that still means it

### 6.8 Rhythm `[COURSE]` M09 L03

> **"Trance rhythm."** Mix short, medium and long — mostly **staccato**.

Run-ons make the brain delete older material; by the time a 30-word sentence ends, the beginning is gone. **Clarity outranks rhythm.** If a sentence has to be ugly to be clear, let it be ugly.

### 6.9 Speed to value `[COURSE]` M09 L03, M04 L05

Think of attention as an **hourglass**. Inside the first 5 seconds you must either deliver value or signal that it is coming.

- **Over-delivering** on the bait early means the viewer has no reason to stay.
- **Under-delivering** means they leave resentful.
- The gate: **speed to value < 4 seconds.**

### 6.10 The value transfer chain `[COURSE]` M09 L02

```
TAX → SAMPLE → DRIP → TRANSFER → RETAINED
```

Five states, each with its own failure mode. A viewer "pays a tax" (their attention) to get a sample of your value, receives it in drips, transfers it into their own situation, and retains it. If any state fails, the chain breaks and the viewer leaves at that state — and the fix is different for each.

### 6.11 CTA doctrine `[COURSE]` M09 L06

> **Run a CTA on every video. Never break hypnosis.**

- Native reference — it should sound like part of the content
- Product as the answer to the problem just raised
- Placement by flow, not by formula

### 6.12 CTA ranking `[COURSE]` M09 L06

```
FOLLOW  (Value Promise > Transformation Story > Avatar-Targeted)
   >    ENGAGEMENT  (Save > Tag > Comment)
   >    LEAD
```

**Default = Value Promise.** "Follow for the 90 days it takes to actually finish one thing" beats "follow for more tips" because it names a specific transformation and a specific reason.

**One ask per video.** Three asks gets none.

### 6.13 The scriptwriting workflow `[COURSE]` M09 L07

```
1. format            ┐
2. premise           │
3. gather substance  │  six steps of THINKING
4. most interesting  │
5. common belief     │
6. contrast          ┘
7. draft              ←  one step of WRITING
```

The ratio is the lesson. Most people invert it: they draft immediately and call it "writing," when what they are actually doing is skipping the thinking that makes the draft worth writing.

### 6.14 Script quality gates `[DERIVED]` — a working checklist

Run this before recording. One "no" means do not record.

- [ ] Hook lands in **one sentence**, ≤ 12 words
- [ ] Visual hook exists **without** the audio
- [ ] Value arrives before **4 seconds**
- [ ] One nugget only — can you state it in one line?
- [ ] Every sentence passes the **$100 test**
- [ ] One concession before the contrarian claim
- [ ] Key point appears **twice**, differently phrased
- [ ] One CTA, Follow-first
- [ ] Read aloud: **no stumbles, no run-ons**
- [ ] Nothing claimed that you cannot source (`[COURSE]` / `[DERIVED]` / `[UNVERIFIED]`)

## §7 · FORMAT AND STRUCTURE

### 7.1 The ten Lego Bricks `[COURSE]` M04 L04–05

Every video decomposes into:

**Format · Topic · Seed · Substance · Spoken/Visual/Text Hook · Story Structure · CTA · Visual Layout · Elements**

The value of the decomposition is diagnostic. When a video underperforms, you do not say "it was bad" — you say **which brick failed**. Almost always the hook bricks, occasionally substance, almost never the format.

### 7.2 The format library `[COURSE]` M04 L04–05

**12 educational formats + 8 storytelling formats = 20 total.** Each maps 1:1 to a named story structure, with beats specified.

### 7.3 The five formats used in practice `[DERIVED]` — a working set

Distilled from the full library for a single-operator faceless channel. These are the five that carry a channel, with the associated cost:

| Code | Format | Length | Structure | Production cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **F1** | **Slab** — one argument, one reframe | 25–35 s | 6 stills, parallax, one caption line per still | Lowest |
| **F2** | **Breakdown** — a system in steps | 45–65 s | 9 stills, step counter, pill karaoke | Medium |
| **F3** | **Case study** — a story with a number | 70–90 s | 8 stills, 4 beats, slow push-in | **Highest — requires lived evidence** |
| **F4** | **Essay** — consequences and evidence | 35–45 s | 7 stills, dark grade, slow cuts | Medium |
| **F5** | **Blueprint** — a screenshot-able asset | 55–70 s | 10 stills, template mockups | Medium |

**Target mix:** 40 / 20 / 20 / 10 / 10 (F1 / F2 / F3 / F4 / F5).

**The structural trap** `[DERIVED]`: **F1 is the only format you can produce without having done anything.** So a bank written ahead of the evidence drifts to F1 by default — a real 104-topic bank came out 48% F1, 6% F3, 6% F5. That is not a taste problem, it is what a bank looks like before the evidence exists. Fix it with §4.5's proof engine, not with more topic writing.

## §8 · PRODUCTION

### 8.1 The production sequence with gates `[COURSE]` M04 L05

Already stated in §1.2. The gates are the point:

| Gate | Passes when |
|---|---|
| After pre-scripting | The topic has a validated outlier or a lived receipt |
| After scripting | The §6.14 checklist is all-yes |
| After recording | Speed to value < 4 s; no stumbles |
| After editing | Hook aligned across all four components; pacing test passes (§8.4) |
| After posting | One variable changed from the last video, not five |
| After analysing | A decision is written down, not a feeling |

### 8.2 Quality gates `[COURSE]` M04 L05

- **Speed to value < 4 s**
- **Shock assessment ≥ 90/100**
- **All three (four) hooks aligned** and data-validated
- **10× outlier = a win**
- **One focus per cycle**

### 8.3 Editing strategy `[COURSE]` M11 L04

**Strategy vs execution** `[COURSE]` M11 L02 — the strategic question ("what is this video doing to the viewer's attention?") precedes the executional one ("which transition?"). Almost all amateur editing arguments are executional arguments masking a strategic vacuum.

**The 45 mph rule** `[COURSE]` M11 L04 — the video should feel like it is moving at a pace that does not let the eye settle but never feels frantic. A useful calibration: if you cannot read the caption at normal speed, it is too fast; if you notice the still, it is too slow.

**The visual ladder** `[COURSE]` M11 L04 — visual interest should escalate across the video, not stay flat. A video that is visually identical at 2 s and 40 s has taught the viewer that nothing is coming.

> ⚠️ **Confidence note:** M11 L04 is described in the course index as containing "the 4 edit targets." I can state the three elements above with confidence; the fourth target is not reliably reconstructed here. Do not assert a specific fourth target without re-reading the record.

**Copywork** `[COURSE]` M11 L02 — rebuild an existing video shot-for-shot as training. The fastest way to acquire an editing grammar, and it is explicitly recommended rather than treated as cheating.

### 8.4 The pacing test `[COURSE]` M04 L05, M11 L06

> **"Close your eyes and listen — if you get bored, cut more finely."**

Audio-only is the honest test. Visuals flatter bad pacing; when you remove them, a sagging middle becomes obvious in about four seconds.

### 8.5 Pebbles and sand `[COURSE]` M11 L06

Structure your timeline as **large elements first (pebbles), then small (sand) filling the gaps** — rather than starting with detail. The metaphor is a jar: sand first and no pebbles fit. The production consequence is that you place the major beats before you place any ornament.

### 8.6 Caption specification `[COURSE]` M11 L06 + `[DERIVED]` calibration

From the course: captions are a **key visual**, not accessibility. They are one of the primary attention mechanisms.

Working spec `[DERIVED]`, calibrated for vertical short-form:

| Property | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Size** | 5–6% of screen height | Below this, unreadable at scroll speed; above, it covers the subject |
| **Words** | Max 8 per caption | Hitting the 8-word limit means the line should have been two |
| **Lead** | Text leads VO by **100–200 ms** | Reading is faster than listening; if text lags, it feels wrong without the viewer knowing why |
| **Hold** | 200–400 ms after the line ends | Gives the eye time to complete the word |
| **Style** | Weight on one word per caption | Emphasis is meaningless if everything is emphasised |
| **Placement** | Respect a reserved third | Reserve it at *generation* time, not at edit time (§11.5) |

### 8.7 Eye level variation `[COURSE]` M11 L06

Vary the camera's relationship to the subject across a video — high, eye-level, low. A video shot entirely at eye level reads as flat regardless of how good the content is. This is one of the cheapest and most-neglected improvements available.

### 8.8 Signature style `[COURSE]` M11 L08

Four components, plus an **editor briefing protocol** and **31 layouts**. The protocol matters more than the components: a signature style is only a signature if it can be **briefed to someone else** and reproduced. If it lives only in your head, it is a habit, not a style.

### 8.9 The six-step editing workflow `[COURSE]` M11 L07

Six steps, with **cadence numbers** specified and a **−14 LUFS** export target. The loudness number is not a suggestion — platforms normalise, and an over-hot mix gets turned down and sounds worse than a correctly-levelled one.

### 8.10 Audio, in practice `[DERIVED]`

- **Music duck −14 to −18 dB** under narration. Not −8. A −8 dB duck leaves the music audible at exactly the moments the voice needs to be alone.
- **The strongest audio device is a silence.** Drop the bed entirely under the single most important line; the absence does more work than any riser.
- **One accent, not twelve.** A beat accent on every cut is noise. One, on the turn.
- **Normalise to −14 LUFS** before delivery.
- **No sound effect under the hook.** Let the first 3 seconds be a voice, not a whoosh.

## §10 · ANALYTICS AND ITERATION

### 10.1 What to measure `[COURSE]` M04 L05

- **$$ per view**
- **Followers per view**
- **Total views vs channel baseline**
- **10× outlier check**
- **Single focus**

Note what is *not* on the list: likes. Likes are a courtesy metric — they correlate with nothing you can spend.

### 10.2 The comparison that matters `[COURSE]` M07 L03

**Against your own baseline, never against absolute numbers.** A 20K view video on a 2K-view channel is a 10× outlier and the most valuable data point you will get that month. The same 20K on a 200K channel is a failure. Absolute view counts are a vanity trap that teaches nothing.

### 10.3 One focus per cycle `[COURSE]` M04 L05

Change **one** variable per cycle. Change five and you learn nothing, because you cannot attribute the result. This is the most-disobeyed rule in the discipline, because changing one thing feels too slow — and changing five feels productive right up until month six, when you have no idea what works.

### 10.4 A retention read `[DERIVED]`

Standard diagnostic order for a short-form video:

| Drop-off location | Diagnosis | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| **0–3 s** | Hook failed to hold | Rewrite hook; check visual hook exists |
| **3–8 s** | Value arrived too late | Move the payoff to sentence one |
| **Middle** | No headfakes; body is a list | Insert a concession or a contrast at each third |
| **After the turn** | Payoff over-delivered; nothing left | Shorten; move the reveal later |
| **Last 5 s** | CTA too weak or too many asks | One ask, Follow-first (§6.12) |

## §11 · THE PRODUCTION STACK — what the tools can and cannot do

`[DERIVED]` — this section is not course material. It is an operational map of a motion-graphics production system (HyperFrames), verified entry-by-entry against its own capability index: **1549 scene entries, 456 registry components, 27 chapters, 3h 00m runtime.**

### 11.1 The pipeline, in order

```
1. STILLS        generate cinematic 3D images (3 depth planes — see §11.5)
2. CUTOUT        remove-background → isolate the midground subject
3. DEPTH         assemble foreground / midground / background plates
4. ANIMATE       named component moves, per shot
5. CAPTIONS      editorial style, word-timed
6. VO            record or TTS
7. MUSIC         bed + ducking
8. SFX           sparse, and never under the hook
9. MASTER        normalize → −14 LUFS → render
```

### 11.2 Camera and depth components — the ones that matter most

`Parallax Zoom` · `Parallax Unzoom` · `Push In` · `Pull Back Reveal` · `Camera Punch-In` · `Camera Rig Depth Stack` · `Camera Shake` · `Dolly Zoom (Vertigo Shot)` · `Rack Focus` · `Focus Rack` · `Focus Blur Resolve` · `Drift Hold` · `Pan Stations` · `Match Cut` · `Whip Pan` · `Zoom Through Transition`

> ⚠️ **There is no "Ken Burns" component.** The term is universal in video editing and completely absent from this registry. The real equivalents are `Parallax Zoom` / `Parallax Unzoom` / `Push In`. If you brief an editor with "Ken Burns," you are describing a move the system cannot execute by that name.

### 11.3 Atmosphere components — what makes separate stills read as one film

`Warm Grain` · `Grain Overlay` · `Grain Field` · `Fractal Noise` · `Light Leak` · `Organic Light Leak Overlay` · `Godrays` · `Vignette` · `Feathered Spotlight` · `Shimmer Sweep` · `Light Sweep Pass` · `Scan Band` · `Cinemagraph` · `Nebula` · `Starfield` · `Frost`

**The single highest-value layer:** one grain pass at low opacity **across the whole timeline**, not per-shot. It binds twelve separate generated images into one film. Without it, the viewer's eye registers "twelve images"; with it, "a video."

### 11.4 Typography and caption components

`Editorial Emphasis` · `Headline Slam` · `Kinetic Type` · `Text Stagger` · `Per Word Rise` · `Pill Karaoke` · `Weight Shift` · `Handwritten Title` · `Marker Highlight` · `Inline Highlight` · `Strikethrough Replace` · `Titlecard Calm` · `Titlecard Lockup` · `CTA Close` · `CTA Lockup` · `Logo Brand Close`

**Which to use:** `Editorial Emphasis` for a cinematic grade — it weights one word and lets the rest sit light. `Pill Karaoke` is a talking-head YouTube look and fights a cinematic palette. Choosing between them is a genre decision, not a taste one.

### 11.5 ⭐ The rule that decides whether the whole pipeline works

> **A flat still cannot be parallaxed.**

This is the single most common way a stills→motion pipeline fails. You generate a beautiful image, then discover there is nothing in it to separate, so the "motion" is a slow scale on a photograph — which reads as a slideshow.

**Two generation-time constraints that prevent it:**

**Rule A — three depth planes.** Every still must have:
- a **foreground** element (blurred, near-camera)
- a **midground** subject (the thing that gets cut out)
- a **background** (atmosphere only)

**Rule B — reserve the caption zone.** Leave one third of the frame quiet for captions. **Enforce this at generation, not at edit.** Retro-fitting caption space means moving the subject, which means regenerating.

**Then:** `remove-background` the midground, push it back in Z, bring the foreground forward. Now the camera can move *through* the image instead of across it.

### 11.6 Audio components

`Voiceover carve` · `Audio buses` · `data-audio-group` · `data-fx-carve` · `data-fx-chain` · the **master bus** on the composition root.

The architecture has four attributes — **volume, filter, dynamics, time (nonlinear)** — and the linter checks them. Note the documented behaviour: **tracks add up; nothing averages them.** Two beds at −6 do not make −6; they make a louder, muddier thing.

### 11.7 CLI reference `[DERIVED]`

```
init · add · preview · render · validate · lint · check · doctor
capture · browser · compositions · snapshot · timeline · keyframes
beats · audio · transcribe · tts · normalize-audio
remove-background · media-treatment · grade-compare · compare · diff
catalog · measure · benchmark · skills · publish
```

> Two corrections worth noting, because they are the kind of error that wastes a morning: the command is **`grade-compare`**, not `grade` — colour grading is a *workflow* (§11.6's COLOR chapter), not a single command. And **`deploy` is a workflow, not a CLI command**; it appears in the WORKFLOW list below, not in the CLI list.

**The four you will actually use constantly:** `preview`, `render`, `lint`, `remove-background`.

**Workflows that exist as first-class concepts:** AUDIO · AUDIO BUSES · BACKGROUND · BROWSER · CAPTIONS · CAPTURE · CHROME · COLOR · COMPARE · DEPLOY · DETERMINISM · DOCTOR · END CARD · HISTORY · MEDIA INGEST · NESTING · PREVIEW · PROJECT LIFECYCLE · PUBLISH · REGISTRY INSTALL · RENDER · SFX ONLY · TIMELINE · TRANSCRIBE · TTS · VALIDATION GATE · VARIABLES · AI SKILLS

### 11.8 Skills and lint rules

**Skills:** `figma` · `hyperframes` · `hyperframes-cli` · `hyperframes-registry` · `media-use` · `remotion-to-hyperframes` · `reverse-any-video` · `slideshow`

**`slideshow` is the one to notice.** There is a first-class slideshow skill *and* lint rules named `slideshow_invalid` / `slideshow_unresolved_ref` — which means the system actively detects the exact failure mode described in §11.5. If the linter is complaining about slideshow, the images are flat, not the animation.

**Lint is not optional.** 153 lint rules encode the system's opinions about captions, composition, adapters and animation contracts. Running `lint` before `render` catches more real problems than watching the render.

### 11.9 Transparency about what this stack cannot do

- It does **not** animate characters or talking heads (`Lottie Character Walk` and `HeyGen Avatar` exist as promo cards, not production rigs).
- It does **not** generate images — that is an upstream step.
- It does **not** fix a flat source image. §11.5 is a *prevention*, not a cure.
- **Object motion** must be faked with camera moves, cutouts and atmosphere — which is fine, because at 3-second shot lengths the eye accepts camera motion as object motion.

## §13 · THE FOURTEEN FAILURE MODES `[DERIVED]`

Collected from an autopsy of failed short-form channels. These are the ways this goes wrong in practice — not the ways it goes wrong in theory.

| # | Failure | What it looks like | The tell |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Hook/body mismatch** | Hook promises a revelation; body delivers a list | Retention cliff at 4–8 s |
| F2 | **Format monoculture** | Every video is the same shape | Saves fall while views hold |
| F3 | **Borrowed voice** | Sounds like a hundred other accounts | Nothing is quotable |
| F4 | **No lived evidence** | Pure stance, no receipts | Comments ask "have you actually done this?" |
| F5 | **Volume without a filter** | Posting daily, learning nothing | No 10× outlier in 60 posts |
| F6 | **Five-variable weeks** | Changing format, hook, style, length and time together | Cannot attribute any result |
| F7 | **Platform-hopping** | New platform every time growth stalls | No channel past 60 posts |
| F8 | **Chasing absolutes** | Comparing to big accounts instead of baseline | Demoralisation, then quitting |
| F9 | **Quitting before compounding** | Leaving at post 40 | The curve has not bent yet |
| F10 | **Production perfectionism** | 3 days per video, 1 video per week | Output below the trust equation's floor |
| F11 | **No capture** | Views but no email, no offer | Growth with no asset |
| F12 | **Funnel-first** | Building the funnel before the content works | Beautiful plumbing, no water |
| F13 | **Attribution decay** | Repeating a borrowed method as your own | Credibility loss, unrecoverable |
| F14 | **Teaching ahead of doing** | Instructing on things you have not done | The channel becomes unfalsifiable — and audiences sense it |

**F9 deserves the emphasis.** The compounding window is 60–90 posts. Most channels die at 40. The difference between the ones that make it and the ones that do not is almost never talent — it is whether they were still posting when the curve bent.
