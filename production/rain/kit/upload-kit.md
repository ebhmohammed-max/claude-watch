## Description

> Rain for three days is an inconvenience. Rain for three days with no house to go back to is a different problem entirely.
>
> In this episode we follow a hypothetical spell of wet weather, then look at what the archaeology of one real camp can actually tell us about how people stayed dry, warm, and fed before houses existed.
>
> At Ohalo II, on the shore of the Sea of Galilee around 23,000 years ago, archaeologists found the remains of six brush huts, hearths inside and outside them, grass bedding, and the plant and animal evidence of a broad diet. We look at what survived, what did not, and where the evidence stops.
>
> Then we go somewhere much older and much drier: Border Cave in southern Africa, where grass bedding associated with layers of ash dates to roughly 200,000 years ago. Two different places, two different environments — not one imaginary camp.

Version 1 was honest and unclickable: a wide grey landscape with a four-word
headline, no subject, no stakes. v2 is built the way thumbnails actually work:

- **One subject, not a landscape.** A lit shelter with a backlit figure inside,
  framed by cold rain — something to look at, and a warm/cool split that
  separates it from a grey feed.
- **A headline that poses a problem**, not a topic: *RAIN FOR 3 DAYS. NO HOUSE.*
  Three lines, four words plus the number, in white then amber.
- **The year as the curiosity hook** — "23,000 YEARS AGO" in the top corner.
- Verified at 320 px wide, which is the size most feeds actually show.

The "Illustrative reconstruction" tag stays, small, top right. The scene is an
AI-generated illustration, so it has to say so — but at this size the qualifier
costs nothing, and no angle or arrow or shocked face was needed to carry the
click.
