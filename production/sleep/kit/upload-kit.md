## Description

It's two in the morning. Something creaks near your front door. You lie still
and listen — and then you check the lock, and the lock does its job.

Now take the lock away. Take the door, the quiet room, the mattress and the
light switch. How would you sleep?

This episode follows one imagined night at Border Cave in southern Africa, where
layers of grass bedding and ash date to roughly 200,000 years ago, and asks what
the evidence can actually support about how people got through the dark. Then it
looks at a study of Hadza adults in Tanzania — a living community, not a window
onto prehistory — and at what a few words of bone evidence from Morocco can and
cannot say about what anyone wore.

No quiz at the end. Just a question, which is the honest place to leave it.

**About the visuals:** Every person, shelter, garment, object, event and
landscape here is an illustrative reconstruction, not documentary footage. There
is no film of the ancient past, and no image in this episode is presented as a
photograph of a site, an artefact or a study participant. The graphics describing
the Tanzanian sleep study are diagrams, not recordings. The Hadza are a living
contemporary community with their own history; the study concerns its
participants, and is not a direct record of ancient sleep. Where the narration
offers an interpretation — ash against insects, hidden clothing, someone keeping
watch — it says so.

Sources and further reading:

## Known limits of this cut

Stated plainly, so nobody discovers them after publishing:

- **Picture is unchanged.** This is the V3 edit, not a re-render. Where the notes
  say "review before release" — bedroom door, lamp and layout continuity in the
  closing sequence, earlier shelter geometry, clothing and crowd density — this
  build did not repair them. The pack's own notes call the door a mitigation,
  not a fix, and that is still accurate.
- **720p.** The stills were generated at higher resolution than the cut uses.
- **Captions are regrouped, not re-aligned.** They follow the draft's word
  timing, cut into clause-level cues with no gaps or overlaps. Wording has not
  been proofread line by line against the audio by ear.
- **No specialist review.** The shot map's own Read First sheet requires it
  before production. It has not happened.
- **Loudness** is −16.8 LUFS integrated, −1.5 dBTP, which is inside the range
  YouTube targets; it has not been checked on a phone speaker.
