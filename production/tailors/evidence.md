# Clothing episode — evidence and claim register

Every factual claim the narration makes, the source that supports it, and the
exact wording limit we must hold to. Built before scripting so the shots can be
routed to the right claim, and so nothing gets said with more confidence than
the paper allows.

Legend: **SAFE** = can state plainly · **GUARD** = must hedge in the narration ·
**DON'T** = claim is not supported and must not appear, on screen or in voice.

---

## 1 · Tool traditions

| Claim in narration | Source | Status | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 62 shaped bone tools from Contrebandiers, layers 120–90 ka | Hallett et al., *iScience* 2021, doi 10.1016/j.isci.2021.102988 | SAFE | 12,000+ bone fragments examined; 62 modified |
| Hide scrapers made from antelope/wild cattle ribs, grooves + polish | same | SAFE | 7 of the 62 identified specifically as hide scrapers |
| Cut marks on fox/jackal/wildcat paws and jaws = pelting, not meat | same | SAFE | Skin removed in one piece over the head |
| "Strong proxy evidence" for clothing, not proof of sewn garments | same (authors' own wording) | GUARD | Narration says "does not prove anyone sewed a coat" |
| Eyed needle from Denisova Cave, ~50 ka, bird bone, ~7 cm | Archaeology Magazine; Shunkov announcement 2016 | SAFE | Widely reported; treat "oldest" as "most often cited" |

| Eyed needle = fine sewing = innermost fitted layer | Gilligan et al. 2024 | GUARD | Authors' hypothesis, not consensus; narration says so twice |

## 2 · Genetic and material evidence

| Claim | Source | Status | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body lice split from head lice ≥83 ka, possibly ≤170 ka | Toups, Kitchen, Light & Reed, *Mol. Biol. Evol.* 28 (2011) 29–32 | SAFE | "No less than 83,000, possibly as early as 170,000" — keep both ends |
| Therefore clothing use began in Africa before dispersal | same | GUARD | Statistical inference from a molecular clock, not a date |
| Wild flax fibres, spun, knotted, dyed, up to 34 ka, Dzudzuana Cave | Kvavadze et al., *Science* 325 (2009) 1359 | SAFE | Fibres are microscopic; no textile survives |
| Colours: black, grey, turquoise, pink | same | SAFE | Plant dyes |
| Woven fibre impressions in fired clay, Pavlov I, ~26 ka | Adovasio et al. 1996 | SAFE | Impressions only — the textile itself is gone |
| No complete garment older than ~5 ka | Tarkhan Dress, Egypt, ~3482–3102 BC | SAFE | Directly dated; survives by accident of burial |

## 4 · The closing argument (§9)

§9 is where the film stops reporting and starts arguing. These are the lines
that carry the most weight with the least evidence, so they are graded here
rather than left to the ear.

| Claim in narration | Basis | Status | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| "They were already tailoring — the tailor is in the tools long before there's a shop" | Synthesis of Hallett 2021 (scrapers, pelts) + Gilligan 2024 (eyed needles) | GUARD | "Tailoring" implies fitted, cut-to-shape garments. The tools support skilled hide work; none of them is a surviving fitted garment. The line is an argument from tools, and is written as one. |
| "Warmth. Then fit. Then identity." | Narrative model, not a dated sequence | GUARD | This is an organising arc, not a chronology. The film gives no dates for the three stages and must not be clipped out of context as one. |
| "once the body was covered all year round, identity" | Inference from beads/ochre at Sunghir | GUARD | Decoration is secure; that it functioned as identity is interpretation, consistent with Gilligan 2024 and with the bead evidence. |
| "the only technology that has to be rebuilt for every single body that wears it" | Rhetorical superlative | GUARD | Not a measurable claim. It is deliberately rhetorical and must not be presented elsewhere as a finding. |
| "a few fibres that somebody dyed turquoise" | Kvavadze et al., *Science* 325 (2009) | SAFE | Turquoise is one of the reported dyed colours, with black, grey and pink. |
| "almost none of it is left" / "the ground eats it first" | Taphonomy; no surviving prehistoric garment | SAFE | Consistent with the Tarkhan floor: nothing older than ~5 ka survives whole. |
| The closing comment prompt ("one hide, one bone needle, no thread") | Direct address, no claim | n/a | A question to the audience, not an assertion. |

## 5 · What must never appear

- A photograph-style image presented as a real ancient garment. None survives.
- Any claim about *which* species made the Denisova needle.
- "The first clothes were invented 170,000 years ago" — the lice date is a
  range and an inference.
- Any implication that Neanderthals and modern humans wore the same thing
  because they used similar tools.
- Continuity claims about modern or historical Indigenous dress.
