## Description

```
We have no prehistoric clothing. Not one shirt. The ground eats hide and fibre
long before it eats bone, so everything we know about what people wore before
tailors comes from tools, marks on animal bones, and — genuinely — lice.

This is the story of how clothing stopped being something you drape over
yourself and became something you build: a cave in Morocco full of scrapers
nobody would photograph twice, a needle small enough to lose between two
fingers, and a grave with three thousand hand-drilled ivory beads on one outfit.
```

```
The lice thing is real and it is the strangest evidence in this video: body lice
need fabric to hold onto, so they only exist once people are wearing clothes
most of the time. Their split from head lice puts that at no less than 83,000
years ago — possibly 170,000. A window, not a date.
```
