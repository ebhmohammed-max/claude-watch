A cold morning, and you own one garment. It is a hide. Not a coat. A hide, folded around the shoulders, held shut by one hand while the other does the work. That is the picture most of us carry of prehistoric dress, and it is probably wrong. A single hide is a bad answer to cold. It hangs open at the neck, stiffens as it dries, and does not grip the wrist or the ankle — exactly where heat leaves a body fastest. So the interesting question is not how someone wore an animal skin. It is when clothing stopped being something you drape and became something you build. Today we follow that change through three finds. Hide-working tools in a Moroccan cave. A needle you could lose between two fingers. And a set of beads that turned a warm garment into a statement. We do not have one ancient garment from any of it. We have the workshop, and the trace.
