Clothes rot. That is the whole problem. Hide is skin: protein, water, and a meal for bacteria. It disappears long before the rest of a site does — so for most of prehistory we are not looking at clothing, we are looking at the shadow it casts. And one line of evidence that is not archaeological at all: lice. Head lice live on heads. Body lice live in clothing, because they need fabric to grip. The moment those two become separate lineages is roughly the moment humans wore clothes often enough for a parasite to specialise. A 2011 genetic study by Melissa Toups, Andrew Kitchen and David Reed put that split at no less than eighty-three thousand years ago, and possibly as early as a hundred and seventy thousand. That is a window, not a timestamp. But it places the habit deep in the African Middle Stone Age, long before people carried it into cold country. Clothing was not an invention. It was an inheritance. Which changes the question. Not, did they wear clothes — but how well could they make them?
