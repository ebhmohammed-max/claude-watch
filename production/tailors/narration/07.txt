This is where reconstructions get confident and the evidence does not. We know the tools, the fibres, the marks on bone. What we do not have is a complete garment older than about five thousand years — and that one, from a tomb in Egypt, survived by accident. We do not know whether a hide garment was a rectangle with a hole for the head or a shaped coat with sleeves. Every image of a prehistoric person in a stitched, fitted outfit is a reconstruction: a reasonable guess built from the tools, not a photograph of an object.
