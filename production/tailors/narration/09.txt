So: what did ancient humans wear before tailors? They were already tailoring. The tailor — a specialist who fits a garment to a body — is present in the tools long before any shop. What changed across those ninety thousand years was not whether people could make clothes. It was what clothes were for. First shelter. Then fit. Then, once the body was covered all year, identity. Almost none of it survives. The softest, most constant object in a human life is the one the ground eats first. What we have left is a scraper, a needle with a hole the width of a hair, and a handful of fibres dyed turquoise by someone who had no reason to, except that it looked good. The next time you pull on a coat, notice what you are doing. You are putting on the only technology that has to be rebuilt for every single body that wears it.
